Accounting & Bookkeeping

Bookkeeping vs Accounting: What's the Difference?

See how transaction recording, reconciliations, financial statements and analysis fit into the wider accounting process.

Introduction

Bookkeeping and accounting are closely related, but they solve different problems. Knowing the difference helps a business choose the right level of support.

What is bookkeeping?

Bookkeeping is the disciplined recording and organisation of transactions. It can include maintaining ledgers, reconciling bank accounts and keeping supporting documents in order.

What is accounting?

Accounting uses financial records to prepare statements, make adjustments, analyse performance and support reporting or tax work. It generally requires a broader view of what the numbers mean.

Key differences

Bookkeeping
  • Recording transactions
  • Bank reconciliation
  • Ledger maintenance
  • Organising financial records
Accounting
  • Financial statement preparation
  • Financial analysis
  • Adjustments
  • Reporting and tax/accounting support

Why both matter

Accounting work depends on the quality of the underlying records. If transactions are missing or unreconciled, reports and tax schedules may take longer and require additional correction work.

Examples for a small business

A small retailer may need regular transaction recording and bank reconciliation, then an annual set of financial statements. A growing service company may need monthly reports, debtor schedules and ongoing review.

When a business may need bookkeeping support

Bookkeeping support can help when the owner is spending too much time entering transactions, records are falling behind or bank balances do not match the books.

When a business may need broader accounting support

Broader accounting support may be appropriate for year-end reporting, management accounts, analysis, tax preparation or decisions that require a reliable view of financial performance.

How accounting records support tax compliance

Organised records provide the information needed to review income, expenses and adjustments. Tax compliance and accounting remain separate services unless the agreed scope combines them.

Conclusion

Bookkeeping keeps the records usable. Accounting helps the business understand and report what those records show. The right combination depends on the company's needs and transaction volume.

Key Takeaways

  • Bookkeeping records and organises day-to-day financial activity.
  • Accounting turns records into statements, analysis and useful reporting.
  • A business may need bookkeeping, broader accounting or both depending on its stage.
  • Reliable accounting records support better decisions and tax compliance.
